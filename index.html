Why jobs vanish

Being good at the work doesn't make the phone ring twice.

Nearly every contractor we call is good at the job. What loses them work is the twenty minutes after somebody tries to reach them — and that part is fixable in a week.

1

They call after six, when you're done for the day

Homeowners look for a contractor in the evening and at weekends. If the only way in is a phone that goes to voicemail, that job is already gone to whoever's site let them book at 9pm.

2

You quoted, they went quiet, nobody chased

Most quotes die in silence. The job usually doesn't go to a better company — it goes to the one that sent a second message four days later.

3

Your Google listing hasn't been touched in a year

No replies to reviews, no recent photos, half the services missing. Google reads that as a business winding down, and ranks you like one. So does the customer comparing you to three others.

Depends what you need, and we'd rather look first than quote a number that means nothing. Roughly: the site is a fixed one-off, follow-up and local SEO are monthly. You get both figures on the call, before you commit to anything, and there's no contract behind them.

How fast is it live?

Site with booking: usually a week or two once we have your photos and details. Follow-up automation: a day. Local SEO is the slow one — that moves over months, and anybody telling you otherwise is selling.

Often not. If it's decent and just can't take a booking, we add that instead. Replacing a site that works is a waste of your money and we'll say so.
